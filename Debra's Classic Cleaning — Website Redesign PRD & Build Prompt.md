# Debra's Classic Cleaning — Website Redesign PRD & Build Prompt

Nov 8, 2026

## 1. Overview

The new site makes Debra's Classic Cleaning the most trusted commercial cleaner a Placer or Sacramento facility manager can find. Every page leads to one of two actions: get an instant price, or book a free Facility Benchmark Audit. Residential cleaning stays, but it gets its own lane.

### 1.1 Problem

The current HubSpot one-pager (screenshots, 8 Nov 2026):

- **Hero says everything, so it says nothing.** "Commercial and Residential Cleaning with a Touch of Class" over three stock photos: a home cleaner, a laundry worker and a sod installer.
- **Stock imagery throughout.** A luxury home, an empty open-plan office, folded towels, a laundromat model. The only real photo is the branded Mazda CX-5, and it's the strongest asset on the page.
- **No price, no path.** No calculator, no booking, no offer. "Request a Quote" sits at the bottom. The chat bubble is the HubSpot default.
- **Anchor-link nav (Work / Services / About / Contact).** There's a single URL, so it ranks for almost nothing.
- **Typography** is a generic serif display face with a default sans body. The cartoon mascot logo reads "home maid," not "facility partner."

## 2. Brand and visual direction

The concept is **"The Inspection Report."** The site looks and behaves like the thing Debra's sells: a facility scorecard. Think clipboards, wayfinding signage, numbered checklists, stamped certifications, monospace readouts. It feels made by an operator, not generated by a template. Every design choice should be checkable against one question: *would this belong on a well-run facility's inspection sheet?*

### 2.1 What we're deliberately avoiding

The common tells of template and AI-built sites, and their replacements:

| Avoid | Do instead |
| --- | --- |
| Inter / Poppins / Montserrat everywhere | A width-variable industrial grotesk + civic body face + mono for data (2.2) |
| Purple-blue gradients, glassmorphism, blurry blobs | Flat paper-white surfaces, hairline rules, one saturated accent |
| Centered hero + three identical icon cards | Asymmetric 12-col grid; hero split into headline + live "scorecard" panel |
| Stock smiling-model photos | Real crew, real buildings, real vehicle; stock only for abstract textures |
| "Elevate your space" / "seamless solutions" copy | Specific nouns and numbers: "42 points. Every visit. Photo-verified." |
| Emoji-style icon sets | Custom line icons drawn on a 24px grid with square caps, like signage pictograms |
| Floating pill buttons with drop shadows | Rectangular buttons, 2px border, 4px radius, hard offset shadow on hover |
| Endless fade-in-on-scroll | Motion only where it carries meaning: checklist ticks, counters, calculator updates |

### 2.2 Typography

All free on Google Fonts, self-hosted via next/font for speed.

| Role | Typeface | Why | Usage |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Display | **Archivo** (variable, width 62–125) | Expanded widths read as signage and fleet lettering; condensed widths for dense data labels | H1–H3 at width 112–125, weight 700–800, tracking −1% |
| Body | **Public Sans** | Designed for the U.S. Web Design System — quietly says "government-grade," which matches the WOSB/SB certifications | Body 17–18px, line-height 1.55 |
| Data | **IBM Plex Mono** | Scorecards, prices, checklist numbers, sq ft readouts | Prices, point numbers ("PT. 17"), timestamps |

Type scale (fluid, clamp-based): 14 / 16 / 18 / 22 / 28 / 40 / 56 / 80. H1 on desktop is 72–80px at width 120; on mobile 40px. Use small-caps eyebrow labels in Plex Mono ("SECTION 03 — PROTOCOL") instead of the current spaced-out "WHAT WE DO."

Derived from the existing logo (magenta and sky blue) but matured for commercial buyers.

Commercial pages lean ink and paper with magenta CTAs. Residential pages bring in more sky blue and warmth. Contrast: all text pairs at WCAG AA minimum; body text AAA.

### 2.4 Layout system

- 12-column grid, 1280px max, 24px gutters; mobile 4-col with 16px side gutter.
- Visible structure: hairline rules between sections, numbered section labels, a thin left "margin rail" on long pages showing section numbers (like a report).
- Asymmetry: headlines span 7 columns; supporting panels sit offset on 5. Avoid centered layouts except for the final CTA.
- Data as design: prices, scores and checklists are typeset like a printed report, in tables with mono numerals — not in rounded cards.

### 2.5 Logo and imagery

- **Logo.** Keep the mascot for residential, the vehicle and social. For commercial, create a **wordmark lockup**: "DEBRA'S" in Archivo Expanded 800 + "CLASSIC CLEANING" in Archivo condensed, with a small magenta check-square mark. Same name, two registers.
- **Photography brief (one half-day shoot, before launch).** Crew in uniform working after-hours in a real office/clinic; close-ups of checklist on tablet; restroom fixtures gleaming; the branded CX-5 outside a Rocklin business park at dusk; Debra portrait, candid, at a client site. Shoot natural light plus practical lighting; slightly cool grade. No posed thumbs-up.
- **Interim** (before the shoot): use the vehicle photo, tight detail shots, and illustrated checklists rather than stock people.

### 2.6 Signature components

1. **Live Scorecard** (hero): a report-style panel showing a sample facility score animating from 71 → 96 with the top fixes ticking off. It sells the outcome visually.
2. **42-Point Protocol explorer**: zone tabs (Entry, Restrooms, Breakroom, Workspaces, Floors, Touchpoints) listing numbered points in Plex Mono.
3. **Certification strip**: stamp-style badges (WOSB, EDWOSB, CA SB Micro, SAM-registered, Bonded and Insured) with ID numbers visible: the codes themselves are the proof.
4. **Price readout**: calculator results set like a receipt — itemized lines, dotted leaders, mono numerals.
5. **Coverage map**: simple SVG of Placer/Sacramento/Yolo/Yuba with city dots linking to city pages (no heavy Google Maps embed on load).
6. **Guarantee seal**: circular stamp "30-Day Walkaway · 24-Hr Re-Clean."

### 2.7 Motion

150–250ms ease-out transitions. Checklist items tick with a short stroke-draw animation. Number counters only on first view. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` everywhere. No parallax, no autoplay carousels (the current site's Past Performance slider is removed).

### 3.4 Quality gate for programmatic pages

A templated page goes live as indexable only when it passes all of these. Otherwise it ships with `noindex` until enriched.

1. **At least 40% unique body copy** versus every sibling page (checked by a build script with shingle similarity).
2. **Real local detail:** named business districts or corridors served, distance and typical response time from Rocklin, nearby cities served.
3. **Industry-specific substance** (combo pages): the protocol points that matter for that facility type, relevant inspection context (for example, California Community Care Licensing for daycares), common failure points.
4. **One proof element** where it exists: a testimonial, case snapshot or job photo from that city or industry. If none exists yet, use a specific FAQ answered by Debra instead.
5. **Internal links:** up to the parent hub and city/industry siblings, across to the calculator and the audit.

Grow the matrix from data, not guesses: each month, add combos for queries showing 50+ impressions in Search Console with no matching page.

## 5. Instant price calculators

Both calculators follow the same principle: **show the price before asking for anything.** The visitor sees a range in under 60 seconds. Contact details are asked only to lock the price, receive a PDF quote, or book. This removes the biggest friction in cleaning sites ("enter your email to see pricing") and turns the calculator into the lead-capture engine.

All rates live in one config file that Debra can edit without a developer. **Every number below is a placeholder** to be replaced with Debra's real labor cost, target margin and local market rates before launch.

### 5.1 Shared UX rules

- One question per screen on mobile, all visible on desktop with a sticky price readout on the right.
- Big tap targets (min 48px), segmented buttons and steppers instead of dropdowns wherever there are 6 or fewer options.
- Live price updates on every change, shown as a range (low–high) in IBM Plex Mono, with a receipt-style breakdown.
- ZIP code check first: out-of-area ZIPs see "We don't serve \[ZIP\] yet — leave your email and we'll tell you when we do."
- Back button never loses state; state persists in the URL so an estimate can be shared ("send this to my boss").
- Every completed estimate fires an analytics event and, once contact is given, creates a HubSpot contact plus deal with all inputs attached.

### 5.4 Pricing logic

**Residential:** base by size and rooms, times the service-type multiplier and condition multiplier, minus the frequency discount, plus add-ons, plus pet surcharge. Range = result ×0.9 to ×1.1, rounded to the nearest 5. Enforce a minimum visit price.

**Commercial:** labor hours per visit = (sq ft ÷ production rate for facility type and floor mix) + (restrooms × minutes per restroom ÷ 60). Monthly = hours per visit × billable hourly rate × visits per week × 4.33 × time-of-day multiplier, plus monthly add-ons. Range = ×0.88 to ×1.12, rounded to the nearest 25. Enforce a monthly minimum.
